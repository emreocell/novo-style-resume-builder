# How to make an ATS-friendly resume

Most mid-size and large companies run applications through an **applicant tracking system (ATS)** before a human reads them. If the ATS can't parse your resume, your experience may never be seen. These tips make your resume readable by both machines and people.

## 1. Use standard section headings

Use **Work Experience**, **Education** and **Skills**. Creative headings like "Where I've Been" confuse parsers.

## 2. Keep text as real text

- Export PDFs with **selectable text**, not scanned images.
- Don't put key information (name, email, job titles) in images, icons or headers/footers.

## 3. Mirror the job description's keywords

If the posting says *"Kubernetes"* and *"CI/CD"*, use those exact terms, as long as they're true. ATS ranking is often keyword-based.

## 4. Use a simple, consistent date format

Use `Jan 2022 - Present` or `2022 - 2024`, and use the same format throughout.

## 5. Spell out and abbreviate

Write *"Search Engine Optimization (SEO)"* once so both forms match.

## 6. Submit the right format

- If the portal accepts PDF, a clean, text-based PDF is usually fine.
- If it asks you to paste text, or mangles your PDF, use a **plain-text version**.

## 7. Quantify impact

Numbers survive any parser and stand out to recruiters: *"Reduced build time from 20 to 6 minutes"*.
